1 · Executive summary

All four Module A briefs are deliberately vague, as shipped. Each was scored for testability readiness (how directly it can be turned into automated checks) on a 10-point scale, and labelled with the requirement-quality defect it most violates. Refinement proposes concrete, measurable defaults wherever the business stayed silent — every such default is flagged PROPOSED for the business to confirm before it becomes a commitment.

Ref Title Readiness Most-violated quality attribute Primary defect label Atomic reqs ACs Tests
A1 Opening an account with ZincBank 3 / 10 Verifiable · Complete req:silent 6 5 7
A2 Signing in 4 / 10 Verifiable · Unambiguous req:unmeasurable 4 6 6
A3 Someone signs up twice with the same email 4 / 10 Unambiguous · Complete req:ambiguous 4 5 5
A4 Forgetting your password 2 / 10 Complete · Verifiable req:silent 7 5 7

Headline risk: A4 is the least testable — the recovery method itself is unspecified ("a way back in"), so no automated check can be written until the mechanism (email link, token lifetime, session invalidation) is decided. A1 hides the most acceptance detail (field validation, initial balance, post-registration state) inside the phrase "ready to use".

2 · A1 — Opening an account with ZincBank

A1 Opening an account with ZincBank Readiness 3 / 10

Read — the vague brief, as written

"Anyone should be able to sign up for ZincBank from our website and start banking straight away. We ask for their email, a password and their name. Once they are finished they should have an account ready to use, with somewhere to keep their money."

Diagnose — scoring & violated quality attributes

Finding Severity Quality attribute Defect label
"Anyone" — no age, residency or eligibility boundary; "anyone" cannot literally mean every human alive. Medium Unambiguous req:ambiguous
No password policy (length, complexity, allowed characters) — nothing to assert pass/fail against. High Verifiable req:unmeasurable
"start banking straight away" — the success state and the meaning of "straight away" are undefined. Medium Verifiable req:unmeasurable
"somewhere to keep their money" — account type, currency, and initial balance are all unspecified. High Complete req:silent
Field-level validation rules (email format, required fields, uniqueness) not stated. High Complete req:silent
No stated behaviour after success — auto sign-in? redirect target? account-number assignment? Medium Complete req:silent

Refine — atomic, measurable requirements

Ref Atomic requirement
A1.1 The registration form SHALL collect exactly three fields: full name, email address, and password.
A1.2 The email SHALL match local@domain.tld (valid top-level domain); malformed emails SHALL be rejected with an inline error.regex default — business to confirm
A1.3 The password SHALL be at least 8 characters and contain at least one uppercase letter, one lowercase letter, and one digit.policy default — business to confirm
A1.4 On valid submission the system SHALL create the customer record and a single default Current Account with an initial balance of £0.00.account type/currency/balance — business to confirm
A1.5 On valid submission the system SHALL automatically sign the customer in and land them on the dashboard.
A1.6 Every account SHALL be assigned a unique, zero-padded 8-digit account number.format default — business to confirm

Specify — acceptance criteria (testable)

  • AC-A1.1 Given an unregistered visitor, when they open the sign-up page, then a form with fields Name, Email, Password is displayed.
  • AC-A1.2 When a malformed email (e.g. notanemail) is submitted, the submission is blocked and an inline error containing "valid email" is shown.
  • AC-A1.3 When the password is 7 characters or fails complexity, submission is blocked; at exactly 8 characters with the required classes it is accepted.
  • AC-A1.4 After successful registration the customer is signed in (session established) and the dashboard URL is loaded.
  • AC-A1.5 After successful registration a Current Account with balance £0.00 and a unique 8-digit number is listed under Accounts.

Test — expand each criterion into test cases

TC-A1.1 Successful registration creates account & signs in — valid data → dashboard loads with no extra login step (e2e)
TC-A1.2 Malformed email rejected — "notanemail" → blocked, inline "valid email" error (negative)
TC-A1.3 Weak password rejected — "password" → blocked, complexity error (negative)
TC-A1.4 Boundary: 7-char password rejected vs 8-char accepted — "Abcdef1" vs "Abcdef12" (boundary)
TC-A1.5 Empty required fields — submit blank → three inline "required" errors, no account created (negative)
TC-A1.6 New account state — after registration, Accounts shows balance exactly £0.00 (functional)
TC-A1.7 Account-number uniqueness — register two customers → numbers differ and are 8 digits (regression/functional)

3 · A2 — Signing in

A2 Signing in Readiness 4 / 10

Read — the vague brief, as written

"Customers sign in with the email address and the password they chose when they joined. If they get either one wrong we should tell them. Signing in needs to be quick and shouldn't get in the way of what they came to do."

Diagnose — scoring & violated quality attributes

Finding Severity Quality attribute Defect label
"tell them" — does the error reveal WHICH field is wrong, or is it generic? Revealing it enables account enumeration (security conflict). High Unambiguous · Consistent req:ambiguous
"quick" — no performance budget, so no assertion is possible. Medium Verifiable req:unmeasurable
"shouldn't get in the way" — success behaviour (redirect target, session length) not defined. Medium Complete req:silent
No failure-handling policy: repeated wrong attempts, lockout, or rate limiting all unstated. Medium Complete req:silent
Email handling unspecified — case sensitivity / leading-trailing whitespace. Medium Unambiguous req:ambiguous

Refine — atomic, measurable requirements

Ref Atomic requirement
A2.1 Sign-in SHALL accept exactly two credentials: email and password.
A2.2 On any authentication failure the system SHALL display the identical, generic message "Incorrect email or password" — never revealing which credential was wrong.anti-enumeration stance — business to confirm
A2.3 On success the system SHALL establish a session and load the dashboard within 2 seconds.performance budget — business to confirm
A2.4 Email matching SHALL be case-insensitive and ignore leading/trailing whitespace.

Specify — acceptance criteria (testable)

  • AC-A2.1 Given a registered customer, when they sign in with the correct email and password, then they are redirected to the dashboard.
  • AC-A2.2 When the password is wrong, then the error "Incorrect email or password" is shown and the user stays on the sign-in page.
  • AC-A2.3 When the email is unknown, then the SAME error text is shown (identical string to AC-A2.2).
  • AC-A2.4 When the email differs only in case (e.g. Test@Example.com vs test@example.com), sign-in succeeds.
  • AC-A2.5 When either field is empty, inline "required" validation is shown and no request to authenticate is made.
  • AC-A2.6 Dashboard navigation after successful sign-in completes in under 2 seconds.

Test — expand each criterion into test cases

TC-A2.1 Successful sign-in — valid credentials → dashboard loads (e2e)
TC-A2.2 Wrong password — error shown, stays on sign-in page (negative)
TC-A2.3 Unknown email — exact same generic error text, no enumeration (security)
TC-A2.4 Email case-insensitivity — Test@Example.com succeeds (functional)
TC-A2.5 Empty fields — inline required errors, no auth call (negative)
TC-A2.6 Performance — dashboard load < 2s measured from submit (performance)

4 · A3 — Someone signs up twice with the same email

A3 Someone signs up twice with the same email Readiness 4 / 10

Read — the vague brief, as written

"If a person tries to join using an email address that already belongs to somebody, we can't let them. We should explain the problem so they know to sign in instead."

Diagnose — scoring & violated quality attributes

Finding Severity Quality attribute Defect label
"explain the problem" — the exact message and channel (inline vs banner) are not defined. Medium Unambiguous req:ambiguous
Duplicate detection semantics unspecified — case-insensitive? whitespace-trimmed? Medium Unambiguous req:ambiguous
No statement of side effects — must no account be created? is any partial state left behind? High Complete req:silent
Does the rejected form retain the user's typed data (so they only fix what is wrong)? Medium Complete req:silent
Confirming an email exists at sign-up is standard UX, but it is a mild account-enumeration surface — no decision stated. Medium Consistent req:contradictory

Refine — atomic, measurable requirements

Ref Atomic requirement
A3.1 Duplicate-email detection SHALL be case-insensitive and ignore leading/trailing whitespace.
A3.2 On duplicate email the system SHALL reject submission and display the message "An account with this email already exists. Please sign in instead."message default — business to confirm
A3.3 On duplicate email the system SHALL NOT create a new account and SHALL NOT change any existing balance.
A3.4 On duplicate email the form SHALL retain the entered name and email; only the password field is cleared.

Specify — acceptance criteria (testable)

  • AC-A3.1 Given an already-registered email, when sign-up is attempted with it again, then submission is rejected.
  • AC-A3.2 The rejection message is exactly "An account with this email already exists. Please sign in instead."
  • AC-A3.3 Duplicate detection fires when the email differs only by case or has surrounding spaces (e.g. Test@X.com vs test@x.com).
  • AC-A3.4 After the rejected attempt, no new account appears and no balance changes on the original account.
  • AC-A3.5 After the rejected attempt, the name and email fields still show the typed values; the password field is empty.

Test — expand each criterion into test cases

TC-A3.1 Duplicate email rejected — register, then re-register same email → blocked (negative)
TC-A3.2 Exact message asserted — error text matches string byte-for-byte (functional)
TC-A3.3 Case-insensitive duplicate — Test@X.com after test@x.com → blocked (boundary)
TC-A3.4 No side effects — original account balance unchanged, no second account (regression)
TC-A3.5 Data retention — name/email retained, password cleared after rejection (functional)

5 · A4 — Forgetting your password

A4 Forgetting your password Readiness 2 / 10

Read — the vague brief, as written

"People forget passwords all the time and it shouldn't lock them out of their money. There needs to be a way back in that is easy for the customer and safe for us."

Diagnose — scoring & violated quality attributes

Finding Severity Quality attribute Defect label
"a way back in" — the recovery MECHANISM is never named (email link? SMS? security questions?). Nothing can be built or tested until it is. High Unambiguous · Complete req:solution-not-need
"easy for the customer" — no usability measure (step count, time) is given. Medium Verifiable req:unmeasurable
"safe for us" — no security requirements stated (token single-use, expiry, session invalidation, rate limiting). High Complete req:silent
No time bounds — token validity period, expiry behaviour, and reset-cooldown are all undated. High Complete req:undated
Post-reset state undefined — are existing sessions revoked? does the new password apply immediately? Medium Complete req:silent
No statement on whether the reset request page reveals account existence (anti-enumeration). Medium Consistent req:silent

Refine — atomic, measurable requirements

Ref Atomic requirement
A4.1 Password recovery SHALL be performed via an email-reset link to the customer's registered address.mechanism default — business to confirm
A4.2 The reset link SHALL contain a one-time token valid for 30 minutes from issue.expiry default — business to confirm
A4.3 A reset token SHALL be single-use: a second redemption SHALL fail with an invalid/expired-token error.
A4.4 The reset page SHALL require a new password meeting the A1.3 policy (≥ 8 chars, upper + lower + digit).
A4.5 On successful reset the system SHALL revoke all existing sessions for the account and accept the new password.
A4.6 The reset-request page SHALL show the same generic confirmation whether or not the email exists (anti-enumeration).security default — business to confirm
A4.7 Reset requests SHALL be rate-limited to 5 per email per 24 hours.limit default — business to confirm

Specify — acceptance criteria (testable)

  • AC-A4.1 When a registered customer requests a reset, they receive an email containing a single reset link.
  • AC-A4.2 A reset link opened twice fails on the second use with an "invalid or expired link" message.
  • AC-A4.3 A reset link used after 30 minutes fails with an "invalid or expired link" message.
  • AC-A4.4 The reset page rejects a new password that violates A1.3 policy and accepts one that complies.
  • AC-A4.5 After a successful reset the old password no longer works, the new password works, and existing sessions are invalidated.

Test — expand each criterion into test cases

TC-A4.1 Reset email sent — valid email → confirmation shown, reset link delivered (functional)
TC-A4.2 Anti-enumeration — unknown email receives the SAME confirmation text as a known one (security)
TC-A4.3 Single-use token — link works once, second redemption fails (security)
TC-A4.4 Token expiry — link after 30 min fails (boundary/security)
TC-A4.5 New-password policy enforced on reset page (negative)
TC-A4.6 Post-reset state — old password rejected, new accepted, sessions revoked (security)
TC-A4.7 Rate limit — 6th reset request within 24h is blocked (security)
